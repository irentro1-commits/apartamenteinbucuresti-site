You saw the listing during a lunch break: new building, bright apartment, the metro a couple of steps away. You booked the viewing for Saturday morning and set off relaxed, sure you would get there easily. The walk to it looked nothing like them, and the walk is the part you repeat twice a day, for years. The listing says nothing about it.

What you gain when the station is five minutes from your door

The first gain is not time, it is predictability. When the station is five minutes on foot, you leave home at the same time and arrive at the same time, whether it rains, whether the boulevard is jammed or whether it is payday across the whole city. You plan your day in minutes, not in optimistic estimates.

The second gain shows up in the evening and at the weekend. With the station close, you go into town for dinner and come back at the hour that suits you, while the car stays for long trips and big shopping runs. For a family, that can mean one car instead of two, with everything that comes with it.

The third gain only appears after you move in: the radius in which you can work gets wider. Neighbourhoods at opposite ends of the city suddenly fall into the same commuting category, and when you look for a new apartment in Bucharest, this criterion weighs more than the colour of the bathroom tiles.

What "near the metro" means in a listing

In listings, distance is almost always measured generously. It is measured in a straight line on the map, from the middle of the neighbourhood, sometimes along the driving route, which cuts through places you would never walk anyway. The listing does not always lie. It just measures from a different point than the door of the building.

Three things make the difference between the minutes in the listing and your minutes. A station usually has several entrances, and the route can drop you at the one furthest from home. Between you and the entrance there can be a busy boulevard, where you wait at the lights every single time, there and back. And the last stretch, the one with steps, an underpass or a slope, feels much longer than it looks on the map.

The other side deserves saying too. Being close to the metro has a price: at the same surface area and the same quality, an apartment a few minutes from the station costs more than one that takes you a quarter of an hour to reach it from. And if the building sits right on the busy boulevard next to the station, the gain can cancel itself out, between noise, dust and windows you keep shut in summer. The combination that holds up over time is a quiet street a few minutes from the station entrance, not the building glued to the metro exit.

How to check the walk yourself, before you get in the car

The check takes a few minutes and you do it from home, in the maps app. You ask the agent or the owner for the exact address, street and number, then you set it as the starting point and the station as the destination. This is where almost all the surprises of a viewing disappear.

  • You put the exact address on the map, street and number, instead of the neighbourhood name or a pin dropped roughly over the area.
  • You choose walking mode and look at the route, not only at the minutes: how many pedestrian crossings you cut across, which boulevard you cross, where you climb steps.
  • You check which metro entrance the route brings you out at, because a station has exits at different ends, sometimes a good distance apart.
  • You walk the route once, at the hour you would walk it daily, and you walk it back too, with shopping bags in your hands.

The map gives you metres. The walk gives you minutes. The minutes are what you live with every morning, the metres stay a number on a screen. If the route works at rush hour, in the rain and with your hands full, then it really is close.

How much it counts at resale, and how that looks in Titan-Dristor

At resale and at letting, the station is one of the few things that stays put. Finishes age, furniture gets replaced, neighbours change too. An apartment you can renovate. The distance to the metro stays exactly what it is.

In practice, that means the filter the next buyer or tenant searches by is already ticked. People search by the name of the station before they look at the kitchen, and an apartment that shows up in that search sells faster and from a better position in the negotiation.

In Titan-Dristor this difference is visible to the naked eye on the same map. Ilioara Residence, at Aleea Eprubetei 7-11, has Nicolae Grigorescu station 5 minutes on foot from the door of the building, with access to the M1 and M3 lines, while Titan station, at the other end of the neighbourhood, is 18 minutes on foot. Frequently asked questions

How close to the metro really counts as close?
The practical benchmark is a five minute walk from the door of the building, because at that distance you use the metro daily, out of reflex. From a quarter of an hour upwards, the walk becomes a decision you take every single time, especially in winter, in the rain and with shopping bags in your hands.
Is it worth paying more for an apartment near the metro?
If you use the metro daily, the difference comes back as time gained and it also shows at resale, because the station stays there no matter how much the finishes age. If you work from home and drive anyway, it weighs less, and the money can go into surface area or a better floor. The fair comparison is on the same criterion: a quiet street a few minutes from the station, not a building glued to the boulevard.
Which metro station is Ilioara Residence at?
