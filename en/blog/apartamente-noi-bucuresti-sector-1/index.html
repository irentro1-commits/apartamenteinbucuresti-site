New apartments in Bucharest Sector 1: where your budget actually goes

If you are looking for new apartments in Bucharest Sector 1, you will get two types of answers on your screen: old apartments with top tier prices or flashy announcements about residential complexes that are just about to appear. The market has its reasons, and they are all reflected in the figures. Sector 1 leads the capital price ranking, and large new apartment blocks have moved toward the edges of the city, where land has a different price. What remains in the center sells quickly and is evaluated street by street, not by sector. We put together two market analyses published in April 2026 and a zone breakdown by an appraiser from December 2025, and then we checked what a real budget can actually secure in this landscape. Each set of figures has its own source, and the money calculation should be read as a budget example.

Sector 1 demands the highest price per square meter in Bucharest

Two analyses published in April 2026, one signed by a credit broker and another by a real estate agency, reach the same conclusion: Sector 1 is the most expensive in Bucharest. The first report indicates between 2,500 and 3,500 euros per square meter in top tier neighborhoods like Aviatorilor, Primăverii, and Herăstrău, compared to the city average hovering around 2,000 euros. The second report focused on quality apartments and draws a wider range, between 1,800 and 3,500 euros, with the exact same conclusion at the end.

Basically, with 2,000 euros per square meter you can buy an apartment in almost any sector, but in Sector 1 you can barely start looking at listings.

From Kiseleff to Bucureștii Noi, the price is divided by three

The appraiser who published price band data in December 2025 reports between 3,500 and 5,000 euros per square meter for Primăverii, with Kiseleff and Aviatorilor ranging from 3,000 to 4,500 euros. Băneasa sits between 1,900 and 2,600 euros in the semi-premium territory, while at Herăstrău the view overlooking the park adds between 30 and 40%. At the other end, 1 Mai demands between 1,700 and 2,200 euros, Grivița between 1,650 and 2,100 euros, and Bucureștii Noi starts from 1,550 euros.

This same sector contains apartments that are more than three times more expensive from one street to another, which is why the sector average confuses you rather than helps you.

Large new residential complexes grow in the periphery, while the center retains limited supply

This is the mechanism that explains why you are searching in vain for a newly launched block of flats in the upper area of the sector. Large developments need large plots of land, and these plots are found toward the city edges, where the final price allows for apartments that more people can afford. Two independent sources, the broker and the agency from April 2026, report the same trend regarding this year's market. The appraiser added in December 2025 that in Primăverii the supply is extremely limited and that the accessible option, with the sector infrastructure close at hand, is found in 1 Mai, Grivița, and Bucureștii Noi.

The agency also noted in the first months of the year that prices in premium areas remained flat, while areas with a good price ratio continued to climb. If you want a new block with a prestigious address, you must prepare for a long search with very few options to choose from.

New properties cost 30 to 50 percent more than old ones, and the difference shows up at turnkey completion

According to the broker analysis, apartments built after 2010 demand between 2,000 and 3,000 euros per square meter in Bucharest, sitting 30 to 50 percent above unrenovated old apartments, which range from 1,200 to 1,800 euros. The difference seems comfortable until you sit down to calculate renovation costs: an old 60-square-meter apartment requires between 200 and 500 euros per square meter to reach turnkey condition, meaning 12,000 to 30,000 euros spent before you move in your first sofa. To this sum you must add notary fees and appraiser fees, and only then does the listed price become the real price.

With these figures in front of you, the old apartment stops looking like a mere compromise and becomes the path to entering the neighborhood with less money than new blocks require.

Rent pays off faster in the accessible areas of the sector

The appraiser estimates monthly rents for a two-room apartment at about 800 euros in Herăstrău, 550 euros in 1 Mai, and 500 euros in Grivița. When compared to the prices in each zone, these amounts lead to a gross rental yield between 4 and 5% in premium areas and up to 6% in accessible locations. Historically, expensive areas have grown between 6 and 8% per year. These estimates remain those of the appraiser from December 2025, and the real percentage will be known after you have a tenant in the house and a signed contract.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Sector 1 so expensive?
Because the land ran out faster than the demand. Primăverii, Kiseleff, and Aviatorilor range between 3,000 and 5,000 euros per square meter according to appraiser data from December 2025, compared to the city average of about 2,000 euros, and large developers can no longer find plots to build here. With the new supply being thin, demand puts pressure on old apartments, and prices rise together.
Is it worth investing in an apartment in Sector 1?
It depends on how you look at your money. If you want high rent, the appraiser estimates for two-room apartments about 800 euros per month in Herăstrău, 550 euros in 1 Mai, and 500 euros in Grivița, with a gross rental yield between 4 and 5% in premium areas and up to 6% in accessible locations. If you hope for property appreciation, expensive areas have historically grown between 6 and 8% per year, although the agency analysis from April 2026 shows them currently flat, while reasonably priced areas continue to climb. Basically, the same sector offers two paths: a large budget with a lower percentage, or a peripheral area with a better percentage and tougher liquidity. The final figure is determined by the specific apartment you manage to secure, along with its floor and finishes.
New or old, what should I choose when the budget is fixed?
A solid old apartment plus renovation costs of 200 to 500 euros per square meter brings you close to the comfort of a new block with less money than a new apartment would demand in the same area.
