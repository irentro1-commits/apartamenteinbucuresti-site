New apartments in Bucharest in 2026: a buying guide

Most people choose the wrong apartment for the same reason: they look at the price and at the renders, and they skip exactly the things that cost them after they sign. The displayed price is the beginning of the conversation, not the end of it. A new apartment is measured in what you get included, what you pay on top and how close the door of the building is to the metro, not in how good the lobby looks in an edited photograph. This guide follows the steps a careful buyer takes anyway, only in the order that spares you the surprises. The figures come from a concrete example, a building already finished in Titan.

The documents are checked first, not last

Everyone asks for the documents at the end, after falling in love with the apartment and after wiring the reservation. That is the worst moment, because emotion has already made the decision instead of your head. You ask for them before you get attached.

What matters, in order: the building permit and the handover report at the completion of the works for a finished building, the land registry entry for each apartment separately and a clean land registry extract, with no charges or mortgages attached to the unit. In a finished building, with a land registry entry on the apartment, you buy an asset with complete papers, not a promise on paper. In one still under construction you buy a contract, and there it matters enormously who answers if the deadline slips.

An apartment without a clear land registry entry is not cheaper. It is only riskier, and the risk does not show up in the price until it becomes your problem.

The state of the site tells you more than the render

A render always looks perfect. A real building site looks the way it looks, and that is exactly why it is worth seeing. If the building is finished, you ask to see the actual apartment, not the show unit: the real finishes, how the doors close, whether the joinery is the one promised, whether the installations are put together properly.

If the building is still in progress, a single question separates the serious projects from the rest: what happens if it is not finished on time. The completion deadline goes into the contract, with a date, not with an elastic formula like during the course of the year. At Ilioara Residence, completion is fixed for December 2026, and the building, ground floor plus 8 storeys, is already built, so the discussion is no longer whether it goes up, but what you choose from what is left.

A written deadline is a promise with consequences. A vague deadline is a side door someone else walks out of, not you.

Included features versus options: this is where the real price hides

Two apartments with the same price per square metre can be thousands of euros apart, and the difference does not show in the listing. It shows in the list of what is included and what you pay for separately. Your own boiler, the type of joinery, the underfloor heating, all of them either go into the price or become invoices you discover one by one.

The question to put in black and white: does the price include underfloor heating in the whole home or only in the living room? Which boiler, what output, which joinery? At Ilioara, the price includes underfloor heating everywhere, including on the balcony, triple glazed Salamander joinery with 7 chambers and a 24 kW Ariston boiler of your own. Things which, if they do not come included, you pay for yourself afterwards, at your prices, not the seller's.

Parking is the chapter buyers forget until they wake up without a space. It is discussed separately, with clear figures. In the same building, a Klauss double space, two cars stacked, costs 25,000 EUR + VAT, a single one 20,000 EUR + VAT, and for the first three clients there is also renting from 80 EUR + VAT a month. Without these figures from the start, your budget is not the real budget.

The area is measured in minutes on foot, not in adjectives

Near the metro means nothing. 5 minutes on foot from the metro means something. The difference between the two takes 20 minutes out of each of your days, year after year, and nobody gives those back to you.

When you assess an area, you look at what sits within a distance you actually walk, not drive. A shop, a market, a school, a park, a stop, a pharmacy. At Ilioara Residence, in Titan-Dristor, Sector 3, the real distances from the door of the building look like this: Nicolae Grigorescu metro at 5 minutes, the Ilioara bus stop at 3 minutes, trams 19, 23 and 27 at 4 minutes, Mega Image and Piața Mihail Ambrozie at 6-7 minutes, IOR Park at 9 minutes, Piața Titan at 9 minutes, ParkLake and World Class at 15 minutes. A non stop petrol station at 2 minutes and a car wash at 1 minute, since we are talking about everyday life.

The street counts too, not just what is on it. A closed street, set back among houses, gives you a quiet a boulevard never has, however central the position.

Financing and the deposit: you know the figures before you fall in love

The expensive mistake is to reserve an apartment and only then find out how much deposit it asks for and in what time it has to be gathered. By then you are already caught emotionally and you negotiate from the weaker position.

You need two numbers from the start: how much the reservation is and how much the minimum deposit is. At Ilioara, the reservation is 2,000 EUR + VAT, and the minimum deposit 30%. With these in hand you do the real sum: how much you put in now, how much you gather by completion, what a possible bank loan covers and in what time. If you are on a loan, you talk to the bank before, not after, because pre approval tells you what budget you have, not what budget you hope to have.

A clear deposit is good news, not bad. It means you know the rules of the game from the start and you can say yes or no knowing what you are doing.

New building versus old building: what you actually gain

This discussion is usually had badly, as nostalgia against modernity, when it is in fact about money and about what you pay over time. An apartment in an old building looks cheaper to buy. Then comes the bill for thermal refurbishment, the installations you replace one by one and the heat that goes out through the walls in winter.

In a new building you pay more at the start, but you get today's insulation, new installations, high performance joinery and a boiler of your own that gives you control over consumption. How many neighbours you have on the landing counts as well. A short landing, with few apartments per floor, means less traffic, more quiet, fewer headaches with the residents' association. At Ilioara there are 4 apartments per floor, one with 3 rooms and three with 2 rooms, on a short landing.

The old has its charm and an already mature position. The new has the saving you see in five years, not in the first month.

The traps that cost you: areas, commissions, deadlines

Three traps catch most buyers, and all three are avoided with a single question asked in time. The first: the inflated area. When you see only one figure pushed to the front, or when the numbers dance between the listing and the contract, you ask for it to be clear what the figure means. At Ilioara the total is given directly, usable plus terrace, between 47 and 84 sqm, with a modular layout, so you know from the start what you are buying, not what it looks like you are buying.

The second: commissions that appear along the way. The question is simple. Is the displayed price the final price I pay, or is a commission added on top? At Ilioara Residence, the sale is made directly by the developer, with 0% commission for the buyer. The displayed price is the price. And the prices, to put them on the table: the 2 room apartments between 107,500 and 136,500 EUR + VAT, the 3 room ones between 151,000 and 168,000 EUR + VAT.

The third: the deadline that is not kept. That one is caught in the contract, not in words. If the date is written down and there are consequences for going past it, you are covered. If not, you are at the mercy of someone else's calendar.

A detail that says a lot about how a project is going: at Ilioara, out of 33 apartments 12 are still available. The lower floors have almost sold out, and most of those left are up on floors 6 and 7, the part with the best view. Whoever arrives late chooses from what is left, not from everything. Frequently asked questions

What does a new apartment in Bucharest cost in 2026?
It depends a lot on the area, the surface and what is included in the price. As a concrete reference, at Ilioara Residence in Titan-Dristor, the 2 room apartments are between 107,500 and 136,500 EUR + VAT, and the 3 room ones between 151,000 and 168,000 EUR + VAT, with the features included and 0% commission for the buyer.
What documents do I check before I buy a new apartment?
The building permit, the handover report at the completion of the works for a finished building, the land registry entry for each apartment and a clean land registry extract, with no mortgages or charges. You ask for them before you reserve, not after.
How much deposit do I need for a new apartment?
It varies from project to project. At Ilioara, the reservation is 2,000 EUR + VAT, and the minimum deposit 30%. Ideally you find out these figures and talk to the bank before you decide, so you know your real budget.
New building or old building, which is better?
The old building looks cheaper to buy, but it brings refurbishment costs, old installations and heat loss. The new building costs more at the start, but comes with current insulation, new installations, high performance joinery and a boiler of your own with control over consumption.
How do I tell whether the area is inflated?
You ask for it to be clear what the figure given means and you compare the listing with the contract. At Ilioara the total is given directly, usable plus terrace, between 47 and 84 sqm, so you know from the start what you are buying.
How close is Ilioara Residence to the metro and to transport?
