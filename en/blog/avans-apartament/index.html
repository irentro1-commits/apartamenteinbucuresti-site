Down payment for a new apartment: how much to save and when to pay

You already have an amount in mind. Maybe 25,000 euros, maybe 35,000. You put it aside over two or three years, with sacrifices you haven't told anyone about. You know it's enough for a home, but you're not quite sure if it's enough for this one, the one you saw on the website and have been thinking about for a month now. Basically, the down payment is the first installment that shows you whether the dream holds up on numbers or just on hopes.

In brief

  • The minimum for a reliable developer is 30% of the price without VAT, and for a 2-room apartment that means over 32,000 euros, liquid cash, not promises.
  • Reservation and the actual payment are two different things, with different roles and at different times. Almost everyone has confused them, believe me.
  • Noua Casă reduces the down payment to 5% or 15%, but requires the building to be completed and registered in the land registry, so it won't help you while construction is underway.

Reservation is not the same as the actual payment

The first money that leaves your account is the reservation fee. At Ilioara Residence, the amount is 2,000 euros plus VAT. What this payment specifically does: it removes the apartment from the sales list and puts it in your name for a period of time. What it doesn't do: it is not included in the mortgage calculation, it doesn't bind you to a contract, and it doesn't guarantee the price beyond what is stated in the preliminary agreement.

The down payment money comes after. It is the percentage of the price that you pay from your own funds before the bank steps in with the rest. For the developer in Titan, the required minimum is 30% of the price without VAT. The two amounts do not overlap, but both leave your pocket long before you receive the key.

Ask from the very first discussion whether the reservation fee is deducted from the down payment or added on top of it. These are two options that change your planning by thousands of euros. We'd recommend you don't skip this question.

What 30% actually means

The calculation is worth doing with a pen on paper, not in your head. The smallest 2-room apartment at Ilioara costs 107,500 euros without VAT. Thirty percent of this amount is 32,250 euros. Add the 21% VAT and you get to about 130,000 in total, but the percentage is calculated on the net base without VAT.

Why 30%? Well, for a building under construction, the bank has nothing to mortgage until formal reception and land registration. The developer asks for a higher percentage to cover part of the costs without relying entirely on bank financing. Basically, it's a market mechanism, not a rule written in law.

If your budget covers 30% of a 2-room apartment, but not a 3-room one, you can stick to 2 rooms without feeling bad about it. We wrote about how to choose between 2 and 3 rooms based specifically on this decision.

Noua Casă reduces the down payment, but with a condition

The program is up and running in 2026, with a guarantee ceiling of 500 million lei. The mechanism: the state guarantees part of the loan, and the bank accepts a lower down payment than it usually requires.

How it breaks down:

- Up to 70,000 euros (purchase price): 5% minimum down payment, state guarantee covers 50% of the loan.

- Between 70,001 and 140,000 euros, for new properties: 15% minimum down payment, guarantee goes up to 60%.

- Over 140,000 euros: the discussion moves to a standard mortgage.

Here comes the part that many find out too late. The program requires the property to be completed and registered in the land registry, with the final reception signed no more than 5 years prior to the loan application. An apartment under construction, like the one at Ilioara right now, does not qualify for Noua Casă until after handover in December 2026 and after land registration.

Specifically: you pay the 30% to the developer out of your own pocket according to the schedule in the preliminary contract, and the program loan only comes at the end, when the building has complete documentation. Until then, the installments come from your own pocket.

We covered the details of how mortgages work for a property under construction in the guide about mortgages for new apartments.

Standard mortgage: what the regulation says

If you don't qualify for Noua Casă, either because the price exceeds the ceiling or because you don't meet the conditions, the standard mortgage remains an option. The minimum required here is regulated by the NBR and differs depending on the currency of the loan and the value of the property.

For euro loans, the minimum threshold is 15% of the collateral's value. Banks add their own conditions: job seniority, credit score, debt-to-income ratio. There is no single answer valid for everyone, and the difference between two banks can be thousands of euros on the exact same property.

Getting a pre-approved mortgage before looking at properties tells you exactly how much you can borrow and with what down payment. It's free and saves you from surprises. We recommend doing it first.

How to save the money without borrowing from family

Three sources realistically work:

Consistent saving. The safest, but also the slowest. If you put aside 1,000 euros a month, a 32,000-euro down payment means nearly three years. Cutting fixed expenses (subscriptions, going out, the second car) speeds up the pace, but doesn't cut it in half.

Selling an existing asset. If you already own an apartment or a plot of land, its value can cover the whole amount or a significant part of it. Selling takes time, but doesn't require saving effort.

The combination. Most buyers do exactly this: personal savings plus some family help, plus possibly selling a car or a small piece of land. It's not elegant, but it's real. And we've seen plenty of people succeed this way.

One thing to keep in mind: borrowing from relatives without a clear repayment term becomes a source of tension. If you can't pay the money back in two or three years, it's better to adjust the type of property you're looking for.

What happens to the money after you pay it

The amount is paid in installments, not all at once. At Ilioara, the schedule in the preliminary contract establishes when and how much: a part upon signing the preliminary contract, the rest as construction progresses. Each installment has a date written in the agreement.

The money is non-refundable. If you change your mind after paying, the clauses in the preliminary contract determine what you forfeit. Read them before you sign, not after. You see, that's why it's important not to rush.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy an apartment under construction with a 5% down payment through Noua Casă?
No. The program requires the property to be completed and registered in the land registry. Until then, payments to the developer come from your own funds, according to the schedule in the preliminary contract. The loan through the program only becomes available after final reception.
Is the 2,000 euro reservation fee deducted from the down payment?
It depends on the developer. At Ilioara, ask directly. Some contracts deduct it, others treat it separately. It's a question to ask at the first viewing, not at signing. We would advise you to clarify it right from the start.
If I don't have 30%, can I negotiate a lower percentage?
With a developer selling directly, without agency commission, the percentage is set in their sales policy. It can be discussed, but it's not guaranteed. For a standard mortgage, the minimum is regulated by the NBR, not the seller.
How much time do I have to save the money?
It depends on the pace of sales. Out of 33 apartments at Ilioara, 12 remain available. The type you want might be sold while you are saving.
