Developer apartment down payment: what the law says and where your money goes

By developer apartment down payment you understand the money you take out of your account before the final sales contract: an amount at reservation, an installment at the authentic promise. Until December 11, 2025, how much the developer could ask you at these two stages was pure negotiation between you, without any limit written in the law. Those who wrote the new rule started from here: they wanted to stop down payments requested from 70 to 90 percent of the price. The rule entered into force through Law 207/2025, published in the Official Gazette no. 1133 of December 8, 2025, valid from December 11. The press named it the Nordis Law, after the company whose insolvency brought the scandal to the front page and rushed the adoption of the law, while in the official text the name does not appear anywhere. From August 25, 2026, the same rules move into article 422 of the Construction Code, through Law 169/2026, and the figures below were verified by us directly in the text, on September 6, 2026.

At reservation you have a written cap: 5 percent and 60 days

The reservation agreement is the first paper you sign and the only one with a percentage fixed by law. It lasts for a maximum of 60 days, and the amount you pay in it does not exceed 5 percent of the sales price. Above this threshold, the agreement is struck by absolute nullity, so nothing you signed protects you anymore. If within the 60 days neither the authentic promise nor the contract is concluded due to the exclusive fault of the developer, he returns your money in full within a maximum of 30 days. In practice, if someone asks you for 10 percent just to hold the apartment in place, he has stepped outside the law by himself, not you.

At the promise, the percentage is negotiation, the account stays under lock and key

This is where the most confusions are made: the law does not write anywhere a maximum percentage that the developer can ask at the authentic promise. How much he asks remains contractual negotiation. What the law imposes is the route of the money: they enter a distinct bank account, in the developer's name, dedicated exclusively to the project you are buying in. From that account the money goes out only for that project, only with the good for payment stamp of the construction site manager, in stages: a maximum of 25 percent of the price for the resistance structure and, after the structure is finished, a maximum of 20 percent for installations. If the money reaches other purposes, the developer risks a fine of 1 percent of the turnover from the previous year, if the deed does not constitute an offense, and there civil actions and criminal liability remain open.

How far does the negotiation go in practice? At Ilioara, the project in Titan Dristor about which we wrote in other times as well, the reservation costs 2,000 euros plus VAT, and the minimum advance requested by the developer is 30 percent. It is an example of a real contract, not a market average, and it violates nothing: at the promise, a cap on demand simply does not exist.

Where the story about the maximum 45 percent down payment comes from

If you read about the subject on other sites, you encountered the figure of 45 percent as the maximum allowed down payment. It is born from adding the spending limits: 25 percent for the structure, plus 20 percent for installations. The legal text however puts a brake on how much is spent per stage, not on how much can be requested, and this is also the most prudent interpretation, made by those who read the law with a magnifying glass. The 45 percent ceiling is not a right of the company, it is a brake placed on spending. So the developer can also ask for 50 percent, and the law only catches the money that goes out of the account for purposes other than the project.

An apartment of 100,000 euros, seen through the limits of the law

For a home of 100,000 euros, the rules are seen in money: a maximum of 5,000 euros at reservation, a maximum of 25,000 euros from the dedicated account for the resistance structure, and a maximum of 20,000 euros for installations, after the structure is finished. These figures are not three mandatory installments and, even less so, a 50,000 euro advance that the company can ask you ex officio; they limit the spending, not the demand. And the ceilings go percentage-wise, so you apply them the same to any new apartment, from those you see in new apartments to any other development.

The papers of the apartment, before the first leu

The promise of sale of a home that is not built yet cannot be signed anytime. The apartment must first exist legally. The building permit ends up noted in the land registry, then the land registry is opened for each unit, as a future asset, and only when your unit also has the land registry extract can you go to sign. The promise is signed only at the notary, in authentic form, and the notary enters it in the land registry on the day of authentication or at the latest on the following business day. The notation does not stop the construction site: the developer carries his work to the end, the only exception being the modification of the surface or the location of your apartment.

The word escrow in announcements and what you actually have

The dedicated account from the law stays in the developer's name, at his bank, with strict rules of use. A true escrow account works differently: it is opened through a separate agreement, and a third party controls when and to whom the money is released. The law does not require escrow. If the word escrow appears in the sales material, you ask for the documents: what agreement was signed and who controls the funds. The word alone, without papers, does not give you the protection it promises.

How much advance banks ask you for, for the same home

Everything starts from the price of the home, which you see in apartment prices, then you continue with percentages. Through the state program, the one known as Prima Casa and officially renamed Noua Casa, the minimum advance is 5 percent for homes of up to 70,000 euros and 15 percent for new homes of up to 140,000 euros, with the state guarantee of 50 percent, respectively of up to 60 percent. At a standard mortgage loan, banks generally ask for a minimum 15 percent advance. The developer, as you saw above, can ask for 30 percent without violating anything. So the hardest cash test of the entire purchase route remains the one at the developer, where the law blocks your account, and the percentage remains between you and him.

Frequently asked questions

How much can the developer ask me at reservation?
A maximum of 5 percent of the sales price, on an agreement that lasts at most 60 days. Above that, the agreement is struck by absolute nullity.
Can I sign the promise directly at the developer's office, to finish faster?
Not there. The law requires authentic form, so a notary, and the apartment must first exist legally: the building permit noted in the land registry, land registry opened for each unit, as a future asset, and the extract for your unit. The notary notes the promise in the land registry on the day of authentication or at the latest on the following business day, and the notation does not stop the construction site: the developer continues his work, the only exception being the modification of the surface or the location of the apartment.
If he asks me for 30 percent at the promise, is it illegal?
No. At the promise, the law does not put a cap on demand, so a 30 percent advance, like the minimum requested at Ilioara, remains within legal negotiation. The cap exists at reservation, 5 percent, and at spending from the dedicated account: a maximum of 25 percent for the structure and a maximum of 20 percent for installations, after the structure.
