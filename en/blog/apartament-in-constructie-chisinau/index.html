Buying an apartment under construction in Chisinau requires different paperwork than in Romania

If you are looking at an apartment under construction in Chisinau, the price is the first good piece of news: for new buildings, listings average around 1,180 euros per square metre in the first three months of 2026, according to the Pinger.md report, which analyzed over 80,000 listings from 999.md, up 7 percent from the same period last year. The Imobiliare.ro index showed an average price of 2,250 euros per usable square metre in Bucharest at the start of 2026, so the difference remains nearly double, and with these figures the Chisinau market also attracts buyers from the diaspora, not just from the Republic of Moldova. The price is the easy part of the story. The hard part is the paperwork: when you buy a property directly off plan in the Republic of Moldova, you sign a contract with a specific name, which the Civil Code covers in articles 1170 to 1176, and you deal with institutions that differ from those in Romania. That is where the difference lies between an apartment delivered turnkey and one you paid for but never see. Below you can find them one by one: what the law says, what documents to look for, how payment works, and how much it costs, area by area.

The Civil Code gives this contract a precise name, regardless of what it is called in the listing

Moldovan law calls things by their proper name: a contract for the sale and purchase of a real estate property under construction, which the Civil Code, in its modernized 2019 form, regulates under articles 1170 to 1176. The developer commits to build and hand over the property, and you commit to pay and receive it upon commissioning. The key difference is that the code covers any arrangement through which a property under construction is promised or reserved for you in exchange for money, even if the paper bears a different title, so changing the label on the contract does not exempt you from the protection of the law. The only situation with a different regime is construction on the owner's own land, where the law sees a construction contract. This entire mechanism is explained at length in an Expert-Group note published through the European Consumer Centre, which you should read in full before signing anything.

Before 2019, the buyer stood at the back of the creditor queue after a developer bankruptcy

Before the code reform, a person paying in installments, sometimes the full price, had no real rights to the future asset. When the developer went bankrupt, they placed their claim at the end of the line, behind banks and the state, and waited. The Expert-Group analysis speaks openly about abuses documented during that period: people left without an apartment and without their money back. The current framework is the legislative response to those situations, which is why every line required in the contract has a story behind it.

The law requires seven specific items in the contract, from the cadastral number to the payment schedule

Since 2019, the list is set by law and looks like this: the cadastral number of the property under construction, the construction permit number with its issue and expiry dates, the project identification data, and a statement on whether the seller owns the land or not. Next comes the quota share of the land rights, the building, and the common areas, the exact calendar date for commissioning, and the total price with the installment payment schedule. Along with the contract, you must request a copy of the building permit and the construction plan. The quota share seems like a back office detail, but it determines monthly maintenance fees calculated within the homeowners association, so a carelessly written line follows you month after month for as long as you own the apartment.

INST released a guide in July that shows you exactly which documents to look for

On July 14, the National Inspectorate for Technical Supervision launched the Buyer's Guide, a free tool available online on the institution's website, in print, and through diplomatic missions, meaning it also reaches the diaspora. The guide covers both commissioned buildings and homes under construction, highlighting what to look for before signing: the extract from the Real Estate Register, the updated geometric plan, the building permit, project documentation, and public registers on the INST website. Sergiu Chircu, the head of the institution, justified the launch due to petitions from people who paid in full and did not receive their homes, buildings with non-compliant documentation, and contracts signed without checking essential documents. The ZDG article from July provides all the details of the launch.

You pay in installments, as concrete, walls, and finishes go up

Market practice, described by the law firm on consultaunavocat.md in a February article, outlines a three step path: you secure the apartment through a reservation contract for a fee, then you sign a legally binding preliminary contract detailing deadlines, handover stages, price, penalties, and termination conditions, and you tie the money to the physical progress of the work, tranche by tranche: structure, exterior walls, installations, and finishes. Reservation has no legally mandated form, so the return of funds must be clearly stated in writing. As safety mechanisms, the same article lists escrow accounts, bank guarantees, and project insurance. You can quickly spot warning signs: clauses where the developer reserves the right to unilaterally modify the project, surface area differences that you must cover entirely without a cap, vaguely written penalties, and a seller who refuses to show the permit or land registry extract. Because this material describes market practice, read it as a map of habits, and for the letter of the law stick to the Civil Code.

From Ciocana to Centru, the price per square metre doubles in listings

The Pinger.md report for the first three months of 2026 ranks the areas for new buildings as follows: Botanica at 1,250 euros per square metre, up 12 percent from the same period last year, Buiucani at 1,420, Rascani at 1,100, Ciocana at 980, and Centru, the city's expensive district, at 1,950, showing an 11 percent increase. The fastest growth is seen in Telecentru, up 14 percent year over year. The old housing stock lags far behind at 820 euros per square metre, so the difference between new and old buildings is felt in every area. These are listing medians, meaning the statistical midpoint of published offers, and the price of the specific apartment you are looking for may differ from these values.

Chisinau is not a cheap version of Bucharest, it is a different market with different laws

Per square metre, the median new building in Chisinau is priced at nearly half the citywide average in Bucharest, with both sources quoted here: 1,180 euros according to the Pinger.md report, and 2,250 euros according to the Imobiliare.ro index measured at the start of 2026, with northern Bucharest exceeding 5,000 euros and the south below 1,500. In Romania, the down payment discussion naturally leads to a mortgage loan, whereas in Moldova the primary discussion revolves around the contract and installments. Furthermore, the legal framework does not transfer: Romania has different laws, such as Law 207/2025 regarding sales of properties under construction, while in the Republic of Moldova the rules are set in the Civil Code under articles 1170 to 1176. Frequently asked questions

How much does an apartment under construction cost in Chisinau?
The median listing price for new buildings, according to the Pinger.md report for the first three months of 2026, stands at 1,180 euros per square metre citywide. Areas range from 980 euros in Ciocana to 1,950 in Centru, with Botanica at 1,250, Buiucani at 1,420, and Rascani at 1,100, while the old housing stock remains at 820. These are average listing values, and a specific apartment may sit above or below them.
What happens if the developer goes bankrupt before handover?
This is the harsh lesson of the Moldovan market. Before the 2019 reform, a buyer paying in installments, sometimes in full, had no real rights to the future property, and upon developer bankruptcy placed their claim at the end of the line, behind banks and the state. The Expert-Group analysis speaks of documented abuses: people left without an apartment and without their money back, and Sergiu Chircu, the head of INST, recalled in July the petitions of those who paid everything and received nothing. The current framework, featuring the purchase contract for a property under construction, with a written quota share and a concrete handover date, is precisely the legislative response to those situations. Your protection is built at signing: request the seven lines required by law in the contract and tie your payments to the construction stage, tranche by tranche, so you do not repeat the pre-2019 scenario.
Does the reservation contract oblige you to buy the apartment?
It blocks the apartment for you in exchange for a sum of money, and the law does not impose a specific format, so what you need to look for in it is clarity on the refund of funds if you change your mind.
