Usable, built or total area: what you are actually buying when you see square metres in a listing

Two friends compare two listings of 65 metres and argue for ten minutes. One has been to a viewing and says the living room is big and everything fits in the bedrooms. The other looks at the plan and counts differently: in his 65, the balcony and his slice of the stairwell are included as well. Both read correctly, they are just reading a different number. It is not a mistake in the listing. It is a different way of measuring.

Four numbers for the same apartment

When you see square metres in a listing, you are looking at one of several possible numbers, and the gap between them is visible at the viewing. The usable area is the floor you actually walk on, the rooms plus the hallway, the bathroom and the kitchen, measured between the walls. The built area adds the walls of your apartment, part of the walls you share with your neighbour and the balcony. The total area goes further and includes the share of common parts.

The share of common parts is your slice of what the whole building uses: the stairwell, the corridors, the lift, the technical basement. It is written into the title deed, you pay for it when you buy and you pay to maintain it every month, even though you cannot put a sofa in it. The more common space the building has relative to the number of apartments, the more this slice weighs in the final number.

  • Usable: the floor inside the apartment, measured between the walls.
  • Built: the usable area plus your walls, part of the shared walls and the balcony.
  • Total: the built area plus the share of common parts, meaning your part of the building's common spaces.

Why two apartments with the same number look completely different

Two square metres with the same name can sit in completely different places. In one apartment those 65 are almost all in the rooms, because the hallway is short and the layout wastes nothing; in another, a good part sits in the balcony and in the slice of the stairwell, and the rooms come out smaller than you expected. The metres have not disappeared. They are somewhere else.

That is why the way the floor is built matters too. At Ilioara Residence there are four apartments per floor, so the landing is short and the common space you share stays small. All the apartments have a modular layout, meaning the interior walls can be arranged in several ways within the limits of the structure, so the metres end up where you need them.

What you can do with the metres outside the rooms matters too. Here the underfloor heating runs everywhere, including on the balcony, so the balcony is used in February as well, not only in July. The rest of the equipment is in the list of fittings, with what is included in the price and what you choose yourself.

How to compare two offers on the same terms

The first question on the phone is short: is the number you are giving me usable, built or total? The second is the plan with the measurements on it, the dimensioned floor plan, the drawing with the dimensions written on each room. With it in hand you see exactly how big the living room is, how big the balcony is and how much the share of common parts comes to.

Until then you are comparing two ways of counting. With it you are comparing two apartments.

Then you work out the price per square metre for both offers on the same area and you say clearly which one you used. Our displayed figure is the total one: the 2 room apartment of 49.2 sqm in total costs 107,500 EUR excluding VAT, that is roughly 2,185 EUR per square metre of total area. The one of 62.1 sqm in total costs 122,000 EUR excluding VAT, that is roughly 1,965 EUR per square metre of total area. Measured against the usable area, the same apartments would show a higher price per metre, so the comparison only holds when both offers use the same basis.

Check as well whether the price is shown with or without VAT, because the difference shows up in the budget straight away. All the prices on the site are without VAT, and the rate applicable at Ilioara is 21%, the standard rate in Romania since 1 August 2025.
